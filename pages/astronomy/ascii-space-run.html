FLIGHT MANUAL

  1. Choose any planet, dwarf planet, or listed major moon and set your cruise velocity.
  2. Select SET COURSE. The distance and ETA use the bodies' current orbital positions and actual kilometers.
  3. Move left and right with A/D or the arrow keys. Mouse movement also positions the ship; phones use the LEFT and RIGHT buttons.
  4. Every shot travels straight forward. Use Space, click/tap the flight screen, or press FIRE.
  5. The time-rate slider runs from real time through one simulated day per second. Greater compression directly increases encounter density, target speed, and score value.
  6. Your cleanup assignment is to destroy as much hazardous interplanetary debris as possible along the transfer route. Each cleared object earns difficulty-scaled points; impacts damage the hull and deduct points.
  7. Before every landing, RED ALERT announces Mr. Dixon's giant ASCII dreadnought. Dodge its dense downward laser barrage, track the moving ship, and destroy it with 10 x encounter-number hits. Every boss hit is worth 1.5 x one normal debris target.
  8. The “MR. DIXON DESTROYED” counter records successful boss encounters across consecutive missions.
  9. Arrival reports actual simulated travel days and hours. Choose another destination while the planets continue orbiting.

DESTINATION SERVICES: After arrival, buy hull repairs. Each 1% restored costs exactly the current point value of one normal debris target. Enter your pilot name to save the score in this browser's local Top 10.

Model note: Planet positions use circular, coplanar mean orbits advanced from J2000. At departure, the navigation computer iteratively predicts the destination's future position, fits a curved transfer arc tangent to both orbital directions, and freezes that intercept path. It is an educational powered-transfer approximation, not mission-navigation software.
